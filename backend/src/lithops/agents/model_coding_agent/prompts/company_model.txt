## Uncertainty is mandatory

`fit` must estimate the model's own residual scale from `history` rather than assume certainty.
Compute one-step-ahead residuals of the quantity you predict (for example successive weekly cash
changes), take their mean absolute value, and apply a floor so a short history cannot claim
perfect knowledge:

`residual_sigma = max(0.05 * mean_absolute_weekly_cash_change, 1.0)`

Return `residual_sigma` in the fitted dictionary and read it back in `predict`.

`predict` must apply that scale as per-rollout noise, deriving the draw index from `rollout_index`
(and the horizon position when a horizon-dependent shock is appropriate) so different rollouts get
different draws. Scale the shock with the horizon, because uncertainty grows with distance. For
example:

`shock = normal01(seed, rollout_index * 16 + horizon_position) * residual_sigma * weeks ** 0.5`

Apply the shock so the accounting bridge still reconciles exactly: move it through a bridge leg
such as `other_outflows` or `recognized_revenue` and let `ending_cash` follow from the bridge.
Never add a shock to `ending_cash` alone, and never let a shock drive counts, costs, or churn
outside their valid ranges.

`predict` must emit exactly one sample per rollout index and horizon, and for any horizon the
`cash` values must not all be identical when `n_samples` is at least two. Every sample must contain:
`rollout_index`, `horizon_days`, `cash`, `revenue_weekly`, `customers`, `churn_rate`, and an
`accounting` object. Accounting must use the exact horizon and reconcile ending cash from:
`period_days`, `starting_cash`, `recognized_revenue`, `other_inflows`, `operating_cost`,
`operations_spend`, `marketing_spend`, `development_spend`, `other_outflows`, `ending_cash`, and
`currency` (`USD`). Counts and costs cannot be negative; churn is in [0,1]. Temporary marketing
spend must stop after `experiment_duration_weeks`; temporary development spend must stop after
`development_spend_duration_weeks`.

Include structured tests that exercise fit and predict with realistic weekly values, plus explicit
limitations. Prefer a compact causal model with fitted parameters over a large simulator rewrite.
Do not claim causality unsupported by the challenge history.
